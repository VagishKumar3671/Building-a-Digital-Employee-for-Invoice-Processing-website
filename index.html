Validation Checks at a Glance

Invoice Date Sanity
Future-dated or stale (>180 days) → Flag
Critical Fields
Invoice #, vendor name, total amount must exist
Credit Note Detection
Negative amount / CN- prefix / "credit" keyword
Duplicate Detection
Invoice # + vendor fuzzy match at 90% threshold
Vendor Verification
Fuzzy match vendor master (85%) + approval status
PO Matching
Direct lookup → fuzzy inference → closed PO detect
Amount Comparison
<5% → Approve · 5–10% → Flag · >10% → Reject
Cumulative Invoicing
Tracks running total per PO across multiple invoices

08 — Customer Communication

When a finance manager asks:
"Why was this invoice rejected?"

A system that can't explain itself to a non-technical person isn't production-ready. Every decision must translate into language a CFO, auditor, or vendor manager can act on.

Scenario — amount variance rejection
Finance manager
"Why was the Delta Logistics invoice rejected? They're a regular vendor."
System output — what they see
Invoice #INV-2045 from Delta Logistics has been rejected.

The invoiced amount of ₹1,18,000 is 12.3% above the approved PO value of ₹1,05,000. Our tolerance threshold is 10%.

A draft email to the vendor has been generated requesting a corrected invoice.
CHECK FAILED → AMOUNT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED · Confidence: 97%
Manager — follow-up
"We actually approved a higher rate last week verbally. Can we approve it anyway?"
System response
Yes — you can approve this as an authorised exception.

Your decision will be logged with your name, the variance amount (12.3%), and your reason. It will appear in the full audit trail and is reviewable by your finance controller at any time.

If this vendor regularly invoices above PO value, I can save a rule to raise the tolerance threshold for them specifically — so this conversation never needs to happen again.
What this demonstrates
01
AI explains in business language
Not "confidence score: 0.43" — but "12.3% above approved PO, threshold is 10%." A CFO doesn't need to understand the model. They need to understand the decision.
02
Human always has final authority
The system rejects — but the manager can override with a single click. The AI never removes human judgment. It makes human judgment faster and more informed.
03
Every override becomes a learning rule
The manager's exception gets saved as a vendor-specific tolerance rule — so this conversation never needs to happen again. That is how institutional knowledge stops disappearing.
